Step 5 of 7
Make it sharp enough to print

    
ChatGPT always saves images at 1024 × 1536 pixels, no matter what you ask for, and that's too small for a crisp A4 print. An upscaler adds the missing detail. You want at least 2480 pixels wide (that's A4 at 300 dpi).

    
    
      
  1. Open a free upscaler. imgupscaler.com works in the browser with nothing to install and has given good results. Upscayl is a free app for Windows and Mac if you'd rather work offline.
    2. 
      
  2. Load the image from ChatGPT and choose 4× (3× also works; 2× is not quite enough). Pick a "digital art" or "illustration" model if the tool offers one.
    3. 
      
  3. Save the result as PNG. Check the size: it should be around 2500 pixels wide or more.
    4. 
    

    
Open imgupscaler.com ↗

    
Why not just ask ChatGPT for a bigger file? It doesn't help. Asking for "4K" or a pixel size changes the style it aims for, not the size it saves.

    

  


  
  

    
Step 6 of 7
Print it

    
Optional but recommended: drop your upscaled image here and the app will trim it to A4 and rebuild the sheet with clean cut marks and a little extra art past each edge, so a slightly off cut never shows white.

    

      Upscaled image from step 5
      
    

    
Tiles will be cut at 66 × 91 mm, the pocket size you chose in step 2.

    

      
      

      
The dashed lines show where the cuts will go. If they don't line up with the pockets in your image, the AI changed the layout; print the image directly instead.

      

      

    

    
Printer settings

    
    
      
  1. Paper size A4, scale 100% / Actual size. Never "Fit to page", or the cards come out the wrong size.
    2. 
      
  2. Paper type set to Photo paper, glossy, quality High or Best.
    3. 
      
  3. Borderless printing off. The cut marks live in the margin.
    4. 
    

    
Paper and printer

    
    
      
  • Glossy photo paper, 200 to 260 gsm. It gives the colours the punch of a real card and feels right in the pocket. Ordinary paper works but looks flat.
    • 
      
  • A photo printer such as the Epson EcoTank L8050 (six inks, very cheap per page) prints these beautifully. A standard home inkjet is fine too.
    • 
      
  • No photo printer? A print shop can do a single glossy A4 for a few rand and will often cut it for you if you point out the marks.
    • 
    

    

  


  
  

    
Step 7 of 7
Cut it out and fill the binder

    
The trick is the order. Do the inside cuts first while the page is whole, and trim the white edges last, so every cut still has its marks.

    
    
      
  1. Let the print dry for a few minutes; glossy ink smudges when fresh.
    2. 
      
  2. Score first. Line a metal ruler up between the matching marks at the top and bottom of the page and run the knife along it lightly, just enough to scratch the surface. Do this for both vertical lines and both horizontal lines. The page stays in one piece, but the grooves are now in.
    3. 
      
  3. Cut the two vertical lines top to bottom, using the top and bottom marks. You now have three tall strips.
    4. 
      
  4. Cut each strip at its two horizontal marks. The marks are still there at the ends of every strip.
    5. 
      
  5. Trim the white edges last. Each tile should measure the size you picked in step 2 (66 × 91 mm or 63 × 88 mm). A hair off is fine; the extra art at the edges covers it.
    6. 
      
  6. Slide each tile into its pocket, matching the layout from step 2. Put your real card in last.
    7. 
    

    
Clean cuts: a fresh blade, a cutting mat and two light passes beat one heavy pass. Scissors work in a pinch, but a ruler and knife keep the edges straight. If you ticked "faint cut lines" in step 6, cut right down the middle of the dashed line.
